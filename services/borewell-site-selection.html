Why site assessment is important

Groundwater availability can vary over short distances because of local geology, weathering, fractures, recharge conditions and pumping from nearby wells. A site-selection exercise should therefore consider the local setting and the purpose of the proposed borewell rather than relying on a single visual indicator.

The outcome is an informed recommendation for further planning. No site-selection method can guarantee the presence, depth, yield or long-term performance of groundwater.

Information to share before a site visit

  • Exact project location and site boundary
  • Site plan, layout or marked satellite image
  • Nearby existing borewells and available depth or yield information
  • Intended use and approximate water requirement
  • Access constraints, utilities and available drilling space
  • Any previous geological, hydrogeological or geophysical reports

What a site-selection review may consider

The agreed scope may consider site observations, nearby well information, local hydrogeological context and available technical or geophysical study records. Where specialist investigation is required, the method, responsible technical professional and deliverables should be confirmed separately before work starts.

The final borewell location, drilling method, expected depth and permissions remain subject to project conditions, technical assessment and the applicable local requirements.

After a location is identified

Before drilling, confirm the landowner's consent, relevant permissions, access, drilling contractor responsibilities and the intended monitoring or testing arrangement. Keep the site recommendation and subsequent drilling records together so that the outcome can be reviewed against the original assumptions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can anyone guarantee water at a selected borewell point?

No. Groundwater conditions are uncertain, and no site-selection exercise can guarantee a successful borewell, exact depth or yield.

Do you need the exact site location?

Yes. A location pin, site layout or marked map helps identify the project area and review available site information.

Is drilling included in site-selection support?

Not automatically. Site selection, specialist survey, drilling and testing are separate scopes and should be confirmed in the quotation or work order.
