Water Audit Reports and Groundwater Auditing

A water audit reviews how water enters, moves through, is used within and leaves a facility or project. Sri Laxmi Sai Scientific Services can discuss water-audit and groundwater-audit reporting requirements, including the available measurement records, site information and technical scope.

What a water audit can review

The scope of a water audit depends on the facility, water sources, process arrangements and records available. A useful review begins by defining the audit boundary and identifying the sources and uses of water within it.

  • Water sources, supply points and extraction details
  • Consumption records and available meter readings
  • Major process, utility and domestic uses
  • Storage, distribution and known leakage points
  • Reuse, recycling and wastewater discharge information
  • Differences between supplied, measured and accounted water volumes

Groundwater audit information

For groundwater-related projects, the review may also consider borewell details, pumping records, flow-meter readings, groundwater-level observations and the monitoring period. Piezometers and digital water-level recorders may provide additional context where these instruments are part of the project.

A report should clearly identify the data supplied, assumptions made, gaps in the records and the period covered. The final scope and applicable reporting format should be agreed before work begins.

Documents and information to prepare

  • Facility or project profile and site location
  • Water-source details and borewell inventory, if relevant
  • Available meter readings, bills and consumption records
  • Process flow or water-use information
  • Groundwater monitoring records and instrument details
  • Any customer, regulator or project reporting template

How the requirement is reviewed

First, the intended purpose and reporting format are discussed. Next, the available records and site information are reviewed to identify the required measurement or assessment work. The scope, deliverables, responsibilities and schedule should then be confirmed in writing before the engagement proceeds.

A report should not be represented as a statutory, regulatory or accredited certification unless the agreed scope and issuing authority specifically support that status.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need flow-meter readings for a water audit?

Meter readings are useful where available, but the required data depends on the audit boundary and reporting scope.

Can groundwater monitoring information be included?

Yes, where relevant and available, groundwater-level observations, borewell details and pumping or flow-meter records can be considered as part of the agreed scope.

Does every water audit report qualify as a statutory audit?

No. Regulatory or statutory status depends on the applicable rules, scope and qualifications or authorization of the issuing party. Confirm the exact requirement before engagement.
